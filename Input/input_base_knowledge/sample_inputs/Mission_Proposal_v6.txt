AGENZIA SPAZIALE ITALIANA
CALL FOR PROPOSALS
PATRIMONIO-CUBE - Preliminary Design of a CubeSat Optical Constellation
for the Monitoring of Major Italian Cultural Heritage Monuments
Reference: ASI-CFP-2026-PATRIMONIO-02

1. Background and objectives

Major Italian cultural heritage monuments require continuous monitoring to
support their conservation, their structural condition assessment and their
protection from urban development, tourism pressure and environmental change.
Frequent optical imagery from a constellation of CubeSats built from
commercially available components can support the national heritage
authorities at a fraction of the cost of larger satellites. The Italian Space
Agency (ASI) therefore launches this call for the preliminary design of
PATRIMONIO-CUBE, a constellation of 6U CubeSats dedicated to frequent daylight
imaging of Italy's major cultural heritage monuments, while remaining as faint
as possible for ground-based telescopes, in line with international
recommendations to protect dark and quiet skies.

2. Constellation and mission concept

The area of interest is defined by a representative set of major Italian
cultural heritage monuments, including the Colosseum in Rome, the Cathedral
(Duomo) of Milan, the Leaning Tower of Pisa, St Mark's Square in Venice and
the archaeological site of Pompeii, spanning latitude 40 deg N to 46 deg N.
The constellation shall have at most 12 satellites, in sun-synchronous orbits
between 500 km and 650 km of altitude, for a mission lifetime of three years.
The number of satellites and of orbital planes shall be optimised to minimise
the revisit time and to maximise the coverage of these monuments, propagating
the orbits for at least 7 days with the Earth oblateness (J2) and the
atmospheric drag. Each satellite shall be a 6U CubeSat with a mass not
exceeding 12 kg, a drag cross-section of 0.06 m2, a drag coefficient of 2.2
and two deployable solar panels; it shall be three-axis stabilised with a
pointing accuracy better than 0.1 deg and point nominally at nadir. Each
satellite shall
carry a visible-spectrum push-broom camera with a panchromatic Ground Sample
Distance of 4.0 m and a swath of 30 km at nadir, acquire images in daylight
only and send them to the ground through UHF and S-band links.

3. Reference operational cases

The design shall be checked on two reference cases over 48 hours starting on
21 May 2026 at 13:41 UTC. In the first case a satellite images the Colosseum
in Rome (latitude 41.8902 deg N, longitude 12.4922 deg E) in daylight, above
10 deg of elevation and with the Sun above the horizon at the target,
preferably higher than 20 deg. In the second case a satellite crosses the sky
of the ASI Space Geodesy Centre "G. Colombo" at Matera (latitude 40.6492
deg N, longitude 16.7039 deg E, altitude 400 m) at night: it shall be above
10 deg of elevation and illuminated by the Sun, while the Sun is more than
12 deg below the horizon at the site. For each case the applicant shall
identify the satellite and the pass among all those of the constellation and
report the satellite, the date and the start and end time (UTC) of the pass.

4. Operations and energy

Each satellite alternates imaging orbits and idle orbits: in a day of 15
orbits, 8 are imaging orbits and 7 are idle orbits, with downlink through the
UHF or S-band transmitters. The electrical power budget shall be closed in
sunlight and in eclipse for every operating mode (idle, payload acquisition,
UHF downlink and S-band downlink), with the duty cycle of each subsystem and a
design margin of 10 percent. The daily energy balance shall not be negative
and the battery depth of discharge shall not exceed 30 percent in any orbit.

5. CubeSat platform design and thermal behaviour

The platform components of each 6U CubeSat (structure, on-board computer,
electrical power system, battery, deployable solar panels, UHF and S-band
radios, attitude determination and control subsystem and optical payload)
shall be selected among commercially available CubeSat products documented
by vendor datasheets, and together they shall fit within the mass and volume
of the 6U class. The temperatures of the CubeSat faces, solar panels, central
body and battery shall be assessed along the orbit and during the daylight
reference imaging pass, and the battery temperature of the CubeSat shall stay
within its operational limit.

6. Verification requirements

Compliance shall be verified by simulation and analysis only. The applicant
shall provide evidence of the optimised constellation architecture and its
revisit-time and coverage performance over the monuments; the propagated
orbit and attitude of the satellites and the two reference passes of Section
3; the electrical power budget of Section 4; the selected platform components
with their bill of materials, mass, volume and declared power; the
temperatures of the CubeSat against the battery limit; representative
simulated imagery of the daylight pass over the Colosseum, showing imaging
geometry, footprint and ground illumination consistent with the acquisition
time; and the light reflected by the satellite during the night pass over
Matera, expressed as a light curve together with the phase angle, the range
and the estimated apparent visual magnitude. The peak apparent visual
magnitude seen from Matera shall be fainter than magnitude 7, the
conventional limit of naked-eye visibility.

7. Deliverables and evaluation

Proposals shall provide the optimised constellation architecture with its
revisit time and coverage; the satellites, dates and start and end times of
the two reference passes; the power budget tables and figures; the bill of
materials of the 6U platform; the temperatures of faces, solar panels and
battery; the simulated images of the daylight pass over the Colosseum; and
the light curve, apparent magnitude and light-pollution assessment from
Matera. Proposals will be evaluated on compliance with the requirements
above, on the technical quality of the design and on the lowest impact on
astronomy. Costs and lifecycle economics are outside the scope of this call.
