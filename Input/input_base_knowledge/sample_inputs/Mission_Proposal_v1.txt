IRIS-IT Mission Proposal for STAR Workflow Planning

1. Mission context and purpose

This proposal defines a preliminary STAR analysis for IRIS-IT, the Imaging and Rapid Information System for Italy. IRIS-IT is conceived as a Low Earth Orbit optical Earth-observation mission dedicated to frequent imaging of the Italian territory in support of civil protection, environmental monitoring, infrastructure surveillance, and rapid post-event situational awareness.

The objective of this STAR run is to build a preliminary end-to-end digital workflow that starts from constellation-level mission architecture definition and proceeds through attitude propagation, power-budget verification, scenario construction, and synthetic-image generation. The analysis shall focus on technical feasibility and data-product generation for a representative optical acquisition campaign over Italy.

The requested STAR workflow is limited to the following modules and order:

1. END-001 Constellation Optimizer
2. END-003 Attitude Propagation
3. END-004 Power Budget
4. END-006 Scenario Generation
5. END-007 Synthetic Data Generation

No additional STAR module shall be included in this workflow unless explicitly requested in a later mission-analysis iteration.

2. Mission concept

IRIS-IT shall support high-revisit optical monitoring of the Italian territory during a five-year mission lifetime from 2028 to 2033. The mission shall use a small-satellite constellation in Low Earth Orbit and shall prioritize frequent access opportunities over the national territory, with particular attention to the latitude band from approximately 35 deg to 47 deg.

A reference starting point for the constellation architecture is a Walker-type multi-plane configuration with 12 satellites distributed across 3 orbital planes. The preliminary orbit reference is a circular Low Earth Orbit at approximately 550 km altitude and mid-inclination geometry suitable for repeated access over Italy. These values are intended as initial assumptions for the STAR analysis; the Constellation Optimizer shall generate or validate a suitable orbital architecture and produce the orbital parameters required by downstream modules.

3. Observation and payload concept

The mission shall use a visible-spectrum optical Earth-observation payload. The reference payload is a high-resolution push-broom optical camera operating in nadir imaging mode. Preliminary payload assumptions are:

- aperture diameter: 200 mm;
- panchromatic Ground Sample Distance: approximately 1.0 m;
- nadir swath width: approximately 12 km;
- nominal acquisition mode: nadir-pointing imaging over selected Italian target areas;
- detector concept: push-broom imaging with Time-Delay Integration where applicable.

The mission shall generate preliminary acquisition opportunities and synthetic optical data products for representative Italian scenes. The synthetic data products shall support early validation of imaging geometry, terrain visibility, payload pointing assumptions, and representative ground-scene appearance.

4. Required STAR analysis sequence

4.1 END-001 Constellation Optimizer

The STAR workflow shall first run the Constellation Optimizer. The module shall use the Italian territory as the primary area of interest and shall determine a preliminary constellation and orbit configuration able to support frequent acquisitions over Italy.

The Constellation Optimizer shall provide the orbital architecture and satellite orbital parameters required by downstream analysis. The output of this module shall be used as the primary source of orbit configuration for Attitude Propagation.

4.2 END-003 Attitude Propagation

The workflow shall then run Attitude Propagation using the constellation and orbit products generated by END-001. The attitude analysis shall propagate the spacecraft state over the representative mission timeline and shall model nominal nadir-pointing operations during imaging opportunities.

The attitude propagation shall provide time-tagged orbit, attitude, access, and pointing-related products required by the Power Budget module and by the downstream scenario and synthetic-data modules.

4.3 END-004 Power Budget

The workflow shall then run Power Budget. The power-budget assessment shall use the orbit, attitude mode, propagation duration, acquisition windows, and representative payload duty cycle derived from the upstream modules.

The objective of the Power Budget module is to provide a preliminary electrical power feasibility assessment for the representative observation sequence. The module shall estimate the energy balance between payload operations, platform standby mode, communication periods, solar generation, battery usage, and orbital day/night conditions. The output shall include the power-budget tables and diagnostic figures required to assess whether the preliminary mission concept is energetically feasible.

4.4 END-006 Scenario Generation

The workflow shall then run Scenario Generation. The module shall construct the representative Earth-observation scenario required for the synthetic-data simulation. The scenario shall include the Italian area of interest, representative terrain or surface information, target scene definition, sensor placement assumptions, imaging geometry, and acquisition context derived from the orbit and attitude products.

The Scenario Generation module shall provide the scenario package, scene metadata, terrain or surface representation, and sensor/target geometry required by Synthetic Data Generation.

4.5 END-007 Synthetic Data Generation

The workflow shall finally run Synthetic Data Generation. This module shall use the scenario generated by END-006 and the upstream orbit/attitude/acquisition context to produce representative synthetic optical images or image sequences for the mission concept.

The synthetic data output shall be used to inspect the plausibility of the imaging geometry and to support early validation of the proposed optical Earth-observation concept.

5. Required inputs and assumptions

The analysis shall use the following preliminary mission assumptions:

- target region: Italian territory;
- mission class: Low Earth Orbit optical Earth-observation constellation;
- preliminary constellation reference: 12 satellites in 3 orbital planes;
- preliminary altitude: approximately 550 km circular orbit;
- preliminary inclination: suitable mid-inclination geometry for repeated access over Italy;
- nominal pointing mode: nadir pointing;
- representative payload: visible-spectrum optical push-broom camera;
- representative GSD: approximately 1.0 m in panchromatic mode;
- representative swath width: approximately 12 km;
- representative acquisition mode: scheduled nadir imaging over selected Italian areas;
- preliminary mission duration for design context: five years;
- analysis priority: technical workflow generation and module interoperability.

Where specific numerical inputs are not available, STAR may propose reasonable preliminary assumptions for user review. These assumptions shall be treated as editable design parameters and not as final mission requirements.

6. Expected outputs

The requested STAR run shall produce the following outputs:

- an optimized or validated preliminary constellation architecture;
- propagated orbit and attitude products for representative imaging operations;
- power-budget tables and diagnostic figures for the representative acquisition sequence;
- a scenario package suitable for optical Earth-observation simulation;
- synthetic optical images or image sequences generated from the scenario;
- a complete set of run outputs stored under the selected RUN_YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS output folder.

7. Scope limitations

This STAR run is intentionally limited to the workflow stated in Section 1. The run shall not plan modules outside END-001, END-003, END-004, END-006, and END-007. Financial costing, lifecycle economics, mission-level multi-fidelity trade studies, and additional optical signature analyses are outside the scope of this specific workflow run.

The purpose of this proposal is therefore to obtain a clean technical workflow for constellation definition, attitude propagation, power-budget assessment, scenario generation, and synthetic-data generation.
