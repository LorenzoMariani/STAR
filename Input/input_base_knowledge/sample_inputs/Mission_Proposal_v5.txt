AGENZIA SPAZIALE ITALIANA
CALL FOR PROPOSALS
PATRIMONIO - Preliminary Design of a High-Resolution Optical Constellation
for the Monitoring of Major Italian Cultural Heritage Monuments
Reference: ASI-CFP-2026-PATRIMONIO-01

1. Background and objectives

Major Italian cultural heritage monuments are visited by millions of people
every year and require continuous monitoring to support their conservation,
their structural condition assessment and their protection from the effects
of urban development, tourism pressure and environmental change. Frequent,
high-resolution optical imagery from a dedicated small-satellite constellation
can support the national heritage authorities in this task. The Italian Space
Agency (ASI) therefore launches this call for the preliminary design of
PATRIMONIO, a small-satellite optical Earth-observation constellation
dedicated to frequent high-resolution daylight imaging of Italy's major
cultural heritage monuments, while remaining as faint as possible for
ground-based telescopes, in line with international recommendations to
protect dark and quiet skies from the interference of satellite
constellations.

2. Constellation and mission concept

The area of interest is defined by a representative set of major Italian
cultural heritage monuments distributed across the country, including the
Colosseum in Rome, the Cathedral (Duomo) of Milan, the Leaning Tower of Pisa,
St Mark's Square in Venice, and the archaeological site of Pompeii, spanning
latitude 40 deg N to 46 deg N. The constellation shall have at most 12
satellites, in sun-synchronous orbits between 500 km and 1200 km of altitude,
for a mission lifetime of five years. The number of satellites and of orbital
planes shall be optimised to minimise the revisit time and to maximise the
coverage of these monuments, propagating the orbits for at least 7 days with
the Earth oblateness (J2) and the atmospheric drag. Each satellite has a mass
not exceeding 100 kg, a drag cross-section of 1 m2 and a drag coefficient of
2.2, is three-axis stabilised and points nominally at nadir. Each satellite
shall carry a visible-spectrum push-broom camera with a panchromatic Ground
Sample Distance of 1.0 m and a swath of 12 km at nadir, acquire images in
daylight only and send them to the ground through UHF and S-band links.

3. Reference operational cases

The design shall be checked on two reference cases over 48 hours starting on
21 May 2026 at 13:41 UTC. In the first case a satellite images the Colosseum
in Rome (latitude 41.8902 deg N, longitude 12.4922 deg E) in daylight, above
10 deg of elevation and with the Sun above the horizon at the target,
preferably higher than 20 deg. In the second case a satellite crosses the sky
of the ASI Space Geodesy Centre "G. Colombo" at Matera (latitude 40.6492
deg N, longitude 16.7039 deg E, altitude 400 m), home of the Matera Laser
Ranging Observatory, at night: it shall be above 10 deg of elevation and
illuminated by the Sun, while the Sun is more than 12 deg below the horizon
at the site. For each case the applicant shall identify the satellite and the
pass among all those of the constellation and shall report the satellite,
the date and the start and end time (UTC) of the pass.

4. Operations and energy

Each satellite alternates imaging orbits and idle orbits: in a day of 15
orbits, 8 are imaging orbits and 7 are idle orbits, with downlink through the
UHF or S-band transmitters. The electrical power budget shall be closed in
sunlight and in eclipse for every operating mode (idle, payload acquisition,
UHF downlink and S-band downlink), with the duty cycle of each subsystem and a
design margin of 10 percent. The daily energy balance shall not be negative
and the battery depth of discharge shall not exceed 30 percent in any orbit.

5. Verification requirements

Compliance shall be verified by simulation and analysis only. The applicant
shall provide evidence of the optimised constellation architecture and its
revisit-time and coverage performance over the identified heritage monuments;
the propagated orbit and attitude of the satellites and the two reference
passes identified in Section 3, with the satellite, date and start and end
time reported for each; the electrical power budget of the operational
concept described in Section 4; representative simulated imagery of the
daylight reference pass over the Colosseum, showing imaging geometry,
footprint and ground illumination consistent with the acquisition time; and
the light reflected by the satellite during the night reference pass over
Matera, expressed as a light curve together with the phase angle, the range
and the estimated apparent visual magnitude over the pass. The peak apparent
visual magnitude of the satellite seen from Matera shall be fainter than
magnitude 7, the conventional limit of naked-eye visibility, and the
light-pollution impact of the constellation shall be reported against this
limit.

6. Deliverables and evaluation

Proposals shall provide the optimised constellation architecture with its
revisit time and coverage over the identified heritage monuments; the
satellites, dates and start and end times of the two reference passes; the
electrical power budget tables and figures; the simulated images of the
daylight pass over the Colosseum; and the light curve, apparent magnitude and
light-pollution assessment from Matera. Proposals will be evaluated on
compliance with the requirements above, on the technical quality of the
design and on the lowest impact on astronomy. Costs and lifecycle economics
are outside the scope of this call.
